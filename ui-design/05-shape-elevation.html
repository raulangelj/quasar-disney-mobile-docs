Decision 5 — Shape & elevation

Two coupled questions. Shape: the corner-radius set. Elevation: whether a raised surface is signalled by a shadow or by a lighter surface plus a hairline border. Palette, typeface, and the reference-tight spacing are locked and identical below; only radius and elevation change.

Elevation is close to decided by the palette you already picked. On a #0E0E12 ground, a drop shadow is nearly invisible — there is no lighter background for it to fall on. Scroll to the elevation section: the shadow-only card is indistinguishable from the flat one on dark, and obvious on white. That is not a taste question, it's a physics one, and it's why the reference signals depth with translucent fills and hairline borders instead.

Implementation cost, on top of that. React Native has two unrelated shadow APIs — iOS takes shadowColor/Offset/Opacity/Radius, Android takes a single elevation number — and they do not produce matching results. A shadow-based system therefore diverges across the two platforms shown side by side, the same failure mode we just rejected in the font decision. Android also won't render a shadow on a view with overflow: hidden, which every rounded tile needs, so each shadowed tile would need a wrapper view.

Option 1 — Reference radii

recommended tile 10 · sheet 24 · field 8 · chip 4

The transcribed values: tiles 10, the auth sheet's top corners 24, fields 8, chips 4, CTAs and filter pills fully rounded. The mix is deliberate and worth noticing — large on the sheet, small on content, full on actions. That contrast is a big part of why the reference reads as a polished product rather than a uniformly-rounded template. Tradeoff: three radius families is more to keep straight than one, so the tokens have to name roles, not numbers.

Elevation — borders and surface lift, not shadows

recommended same in all three radius options

The same four treatments, on the dark app surface and then on the light auth sheet. Compare the first card in each strip.

On #0E0E12 — app mode

shadow onlyIndistinguishable from flat. Nothing lighter behind it for the shadow to darken.
hairline borderReads immediately. 1 px white at 12%.
lift + hairlinesurface.raised #17171D plus the hairline — the overlay/toast treatment.
lift 2#1F1F26 at 16% border — reserved; nothing in Phase 1 needs a third level.

On #FFFFFF — auth mode

shadow onlyWorks here — this is the mode where a shadow actually carries information.
hairline borderAlso fine, flatter.
lift + hairline#FAFAFA on white — barely distinguishable, the inverse of the dark-mode problem.
lift 2#F4F4F5 — the field fill, effectively.

The rule: elevation is expressed as surface.base → surface.raised plus border.hairline, in both modes. Dark mode gets no shadows because they don't work; light mode gets no shadows because using them only there would mean the two modes express depth by different mechanisms — and criterion A1 (re-skin by swapping tokens, touching no component) requires the mechanism to be shared and only the values to differ.

One deliberate exception: the auth sheet's own edge against the gradient. It is the one place in the app where a real surface sits over a lighter background, so a soft shadow is the honest treatment there — 0 -8px 24px rgba(0,0,0,.18) on the sheet's top edge, one shadow token, used once. Everything else uses borders.

Also decided here: translucent chrome (tab bar, filter pills) is rgba(255,255,255,.07) over the base rather than a real blur. RN has no built-in backdrop blur — it needs @react-native-community/blur, a native dependency, for an effect nobody will notice at 7% over near-black. Flagged as a Phase-2 polish item, not a Phase-1 dependency.
