Decision 13 — Motion & transitions

Everything below is animating at its proposed real duration, so the timings are judged rather than imagined. Motion is the last visual decision — and the one that most easily turns a fidelity demo into something that feels either cheap or sluggish on a device.

Data visualization: not applicable. The session brief pairs motion with data-viz; this app has no charts. The only quantitative display is the continue-watching progress bar, which is a token'd atom (decision 6), not a chart. No charting library, no viz palette — recorded so a later reader knows it was considered and dismissed rather than forgotten.

These differ per platform, and that is correct — screen transitions are the behavior half of decision 15's rule, not the appearance half. They pair with the platform back gestures chosen in decision 7: an iOS edge-swipe must drag the screen it is dismissing, which only happens if the transition is the native slide.

This is the one place the demo deliberately looks different on the two devices. Worth saying out loud at sign-off, so it reads as a decision rather than an inconsistency.

The overlay fades rather than cutting for a specific reason: connectivity flaps. A hard-cut overlay on a flaky network strobes; a fade makes a brief drop read as a soft state change. It still appears fast enough (180 ms) that it never looks delayed.

The rule is "shorten or substitute, never remove": stripping press feedback entirely would leave a user with no confirmation their tap registered, which is a worse accessibility outcome than the animation was.
